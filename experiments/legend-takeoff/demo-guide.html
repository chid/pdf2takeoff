From drawing to a checkable price draft.

A three-minute hackathon story: count the items, inspect the evidence, record corrections and apply your rates.

Open PDF workspace ↗Read the full talk track ↗

Presenter setup: use a permission-cleared digital PDF you have rehearsed, or explicitly show the synthetic electrical sample. The sample has three DL1 and two SO1 installations. Rates used in the script are fictional. General Builders and other trade pages provide editable presets, not separate trained detectors.

00:00–00:25

Start with the job.

Before pricing, someone has to turn a drawing into quantities. The goal is to connect those quantities with evidence and explicit pricing assumptions.

00:25–00:45

Choose the scope.

Open a trade page. General Builders starts with doors, windows and joinery; every code is editable. Current scope is item counting and entered rates.

00:45–01:20

Open, exclude, select, scan.

Open a PDF on-device. Mark the legend as an exclusion, zoom to it and choose one example. Run the scanner, then fit the page to see numbered matches.

01:20–01:55

Make a review decision.

Select a hit and zoom to it. Confirm, flag or reject it; add missed installations manually. Explain that supported detections still need checking.

01:55–02:25

Apply the rates.

Enter material and labour per item with a source note. Show how a review correction changes the subtotal. Missing rates and uncertain quantities remain visible.

02:25–03:10

Export, then explain what remains to prove.

Show JSON, CSV and annotated PDF exports. Describe the synthetic pilot and its failures. Finish with the goal: less repetitive counting, with evidence behind each quantity and price assumption.

If matching fails, say so: no matches does not establish no equipment. Choose another example or record items manually. Broad real-world accuracy, automatic legend interpretation, an activity timeline and a complete building estimate remain outside the demonstrated capabilities.
